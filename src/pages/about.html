How the company is built

There is no holding company above us and no sales floor between you and the people who decide where the trucks go.

That structure is the whole point. In a large carrier, the person who quotes your freight, the person who dispatches it, and the person who answers when it is late are three different people with three different incentives. Here they are the same two people, and they carry the consequences of a bad promise directly.

How we work

We quote what we can actually cover. If our equipment is committed or the lane does not fit our network that week, we say so instead of taking the load and hoping. A carrier that over-commits is how freight ends up sitting on a dock at 4pm on a Friday.

We are asset based, which means the truck that picks up your freight is our truck and the driver is our driver. Nothing you tender to us gets quietly re-posted to a load board. If we ever cannot cover something ourselves, you will hear that from us before you plan around it.

What {{ $.years }} years teaches you

Mostly that the boring things decide everything. Pre-trip inspections that get done properly. Securement that assumes the worst road condition and not the best. Hours-of-service discipline even when running an extra ninety minutes would make the appointment. Paperwork that goes out the same day instead of at the end of the month.

None of that is impressive to talk about. It is the difference between a carrier you use once and a carrier you put on a dedicated lane.

What we hold to

Four commitments we will not trade away

Say the real date

An honest delivery date you can plan around beats an optimistic one that collapses. If a lane cannot make your appointment, we tell you before you book, not after.

Price it once

Accessorials get named in the quote. Detention, liftgate, driver assist, and layover rates are agreed before the truck rolls, so the invoice matches the number you approved.

Own the problem

Freight gets delayed and occasionally damaged. What separates carriers is who calls whom first. We call you, with what happened and what we are doing about it.

Keep the drivers

Carriers with constant driver turnover give you a different person every trip who does not know your dock. We would rather pay and treat drivers well enough that you see the same faces.
